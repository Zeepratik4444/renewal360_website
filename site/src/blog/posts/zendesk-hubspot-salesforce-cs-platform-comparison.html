Zendesk is a support desk: it runs tickets, help centres and support conversations. HubSpot and Salesforce are CRMs: they hold accounts, contacts, deals and contracts, and each also sells its own service product (HubSpot Service Hub and Salesforce Service Cloud). Zendesk connects with both CRMs through its marketplace integrations. For a customer success team, the important point is that renewal risk often shows up in the support desk first, so CRM and support data need to be read together.

This guide is for customer success and RevOps teams that use some mix of these three tools. It covers what each one is for, how they connect, and what each misses when it comes to renewals.

Many companies use Zendesk for support alongside HubSpot or Salesforce as the CRM. That is a perfectly good setup. The gap is that none of them, on its own, looks at an account the way a CSM preparing for a renewal needs to.

Does Zendesk integrate with HubSpot and Salesforce?

Yes. Zendesk offers integrations with both through its marketplace, so support agents can see CRM details next to a ticket and ticket information can appear on CRM records. Exactly which fields sync depends on the integration and how you set it up, so check the current options with Zendesk and your CRM vendor.

These integrations are built for agents and sales reps. They move records between systems. They do not answer the renewal questions: is this customer getting value, is the champion still engaged, is anything blocking the renewal?

Why does support data matter for renewals?

Support is often where trouble shows first. A run of serious tickets, an escalation that stays open, or a frustrated reply can appear weeks before the customer says anything to their CSM. If that sits only in the support desk, the CSM walks into the renewal call blind.

  • Open serious issues near the renewal date are a strong risk signal, and a blocker to address before any pricing conversation.
  • A change in ticket tone or volume can mean the product is not meeting needs.
  • A quiet support history plus falling usage can mean the customer has stopped trying.

How do you see CRM and support data together for a renewal?

You have three realistic options:

  1. Sync tickets into the CRM and build reports there. Works if someone maintains the reports and checks them for every renewal.
  2. Use the CRM vendor's own service product (Service Hub or Service Cloud) so tickets and accounts live in one system. A bigger change for a support team happy with Zendesk.
  3. Use a renewal tool that reads both. Renewal360 reads Salesforce or HubSpot for accounts, contracts and contacts, Zendesk for tickets, your inbox and calendar for engagement, and Amplitude for usage, then explains each account's risk in plain English and checks whether anything is blocking the renewal.

A simple decision framework

  • If your support team is happy with Zendesk, keep it. Connect it to your CRM for agents, and to a renewal tool for CSMs.
  • If you want one vendor for CRM and support, look at HubSpot Service Hub or Salesforce Service Cloud, and plan the migration carefully.
  • If renewals are slipping, the fix is less about which support desk you use and more about someone, or something, reading support, CRM, email and usage together for every renewal.

Frequently asked questions

Is Zendesk a CRM?

Zendesk is mainly a customer support platform. It has had sales CRM products over the years, but most companies use it for support alongside a separate CRM such as HubSpot or Salesforce.

Should we move from Zendesk to HubSpot Service Hub?

Only if having support and CRM in one system clearly outweighs the cost of moving your support team. If the goal is better renewal visibility, connecting the tools you have is usually faster.
