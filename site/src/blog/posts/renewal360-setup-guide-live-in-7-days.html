You can have Renewal360 running in about a week: bring in your renewals on day one, connect inboxes and calendars, connect support and usage data, set your renewal playbook, then approve your first drafts and start working from the Action Center. Many teams see their renewal book the same day by importing a spreadsheet.

This checklist assumes a small CS team setting things up alongside their normal work. Paid plans include an onboarding consultation if you would like help.

Day 1: Bring in your renewals

  • Fastest: export your renewals spreadsheet as CSV and import it. Renewal360 checks every row and tells you which columns or values need fixing. Large files import in the background.
  • From your CRM: an admin connects Salesforce or HubSpot once. Accounts, contracts, renewal dates, values, owners and contacts come across.
  • Check that every renewal has a date, a value and an owner. Missing dates are flagged as a to-do.

Day 2: Connect inboxes and calendars

  • Each CSM connects their own Gmail or Outlook, or an admin connects a company account.
  • Before signing in, choose how much past email to import. More history means a better first picture of each account.
  • Calendars let Renewal360 see renewal meetings and offer meeting times from the sender's free slots.
  • Approved emails will go out from each person's own mailbox, so replies come back to them.

Day 3: Add support and usage signals

  • Connect Zendesk so open and escalated tickets count towards renewal risk.
  • Connect Amplitude so falling usage shows up early.
  • Connect Slack and choose where alerts go and which ones each team gets.

Day 4: Set your renewal playbook

  • Choose the steps your team takes before a renewal: value check-in, business review, who signs, commercial conversation, paperwork, thank-you.
  • Mark auto-renewing contracts so they get their own steps around the notice window.
  • For each step, choose how it runs: off, remind me, or draft for me. Leave "send on its own" for later, once steps have proven themselves.
  • If you have several teams, let each team adjust the company playbook within your limits.

Not sure which steps to include? Read how to build a renewal playbook from scratch.

Day 5: Review the first account pictures

  • Open a few accounts you know well. Check that the risk reasons and the readiness answers match what you know.
  • Fill obvious gaps by hand, for example add the person who signs if you know them.
  • Set an email limit per customer so nobody is over-contacted.

Day 6: Approve the first drafts

  • Open Renewal Prep and review the drafted emails: edit, approve or reject.
  • Make sure everyone knows how to pause outgoing email for one account or for all.

Day 7: Work from the Action Center

  • Each CSM starts the day in the Action Center: customers waiting for a reply, one-click decisions, then calls and tasks.
  • Managers check the executive summary and the gaps view to see where to coach.

Week 2: Calibrate and expand

  • Review which drafts were approved unchanged. Those steps are candidates to run on their own.
  • Look at accounts flagged at risk with your team. Did the reasons hold up?
  • Add the rest of your book if you started with a subset.

Frequently asked questions

Do we need our CRM connected to start?

No. You can start from a CSV and connect the CRM later.

Who needs to be involved in setup?

An admin to connect company tools such as the CRM and Zendesk, and each CSM to connect their own mailbox and calendar.

Will anything be sent to customers during setup?

No. Drafts wait for approval, and no step sends on its own unless you turn that on.

Is there help with setup?

Yes. Paid plans include an onboarding consultation, and you can always email hello@renewal360.in.
