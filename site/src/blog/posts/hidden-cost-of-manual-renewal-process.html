A manual renewal process costs a SaaS company in three ways: CSM time spent tracking renewals and writing routine emails, preventable churn from risk that was spotted too late, and missed expansion because nobody had time to raise it. You can estimate each one with numbers you already have; the worked example below shows how.

Manual renewal processes feel free because the cost is spread across people's weeks and buried in churn reviews. Putting a number on it is the fastest way to decide whether fixing it is worth the effort.

Cost 1: The CSM time tax

Every renewal involves the same admin: checking the date, reading back through emails, updating the spreadsheet or CRM, chasing who signs, writing check-ins and reminders. None of it is hard. All of it takes time away from conversations with customers.

How to estimate it: ask your CSMs to log renewal admin for two weeks. Multiply the weekly hours by the number of CSMs, by working weeks in the year, and by the loaded hourly cost of a CSM.

Cost 2: Preventable churn

Most lost renewals had warning signs: a champion who left, usage that slid, an issue that stayed open, a quiet account nobody noticed. When those signs surface in the last month, it is usually too late.

How to estimate it: look at last year's lost renewals. For each one, ask: were there signs at least three months before the date that we could have acted on? The revenue from those accounts is your preventable churn. Even saving a fraction of it usually outweighs every other cost here.

Cost 3: Missed expansion

CSMs who spend their week on admin rarely have time for the business reviews where expansion comes up. Customers who would have bought more seats or a higher plan simply renew as they are.

How to estimate it: count renewals last year that had no business review in the run-up. Compare their expansion rate with accounts that did.

Why this calculation rarely gets made

  • Admin time is spread thinly, so nobody sees it as a single cost.
  • Churn reviews focus on the customer's reason ("budget", "went another way") rather than on when the signs first appeared.
  • Missed expansion leaves no trace at all.

What fixing it looks like

  • Time: renewal tracking and first drafts handled by software, with CSMs approving rather than writing.
  • Churn: every account watched all year, with gaps such as no known signer or no recent business review raised months ahead.
  • Expansion: CSM hours freed for business reviews with the accounts that matter.

Frequently asked questions

How much time do CSMs spend on renewal admin?

It varies widely by team and tooling. The only reliable answer is to measure it: have CSMs log renewal admin for two weeks.

What counts as preventable churn?

Lost revenue from accounts that showed clear warning signs early enough to act, for example three months or more before the renewal.
