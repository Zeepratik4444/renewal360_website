Renewal360 connects to your CRM, inbox, calendar, support desk and product analytics, reads them every day, and checks each renewal for what is missing. It explains each account's risk in plain English, puts the day's work in one list, drafts the next step for your team to approve, and gives managers a live view of the whole book. You decide how much it does on its own.

This is a walk through the product from a CSM's day to a manager's view. If you prefer video, the five-minute product tour shows the same screens on our Ganvix demo workspace.

1. It brings in your renewals

Connect Salesforce or HubSpot, or import a spreadsheet. Renewal360 builds your renewal book: accounts, contracts, renewal dates, value and owners. Each CSM connects their own Gmail or Outlook and calendar, or an admin connects a company account. Zendesk adds support tickets, Amplitude adds product usage, and Slack carries alerts. See all integrations.

2. It reads every account, every day

Emails and replies, meetings, deal stages, support tickets and usage are read together. Each account gets a risk score with the reasons written out, for example: the customer is reviewing their tools, the tone of recent replies turned negative, and usage is low. Risk score movements show which accounts got worse or better since the last check, so nothing changes quietly.

3. It checks what each renewal is missing

Each renewal is checked against the questions that decide it: Are they getting value? Is the champion engaged and replying? Do we know more than one person? Do we know who signs? Is anything blocking it? Has pricing been discussed? Is the renewal process clear? Has there been a real business review lately? Do meetings actually happen? Each answer is backed by evidence. A gap becomes a next step.

4. It puts the day's work in one list

The Action Center is where each CSM starts. Across the top: pipeline, revenue at risk, how much is on your list, and anything overdue. Below, the to-do list in three parts: customers waiting for a reply, one-click decisions, and the calls and tasks that matter most, each with days left until renewal.

5. It drafts the next step, and you approve it

Renewal360 drafts the outreach each gap calls for: the check-in, the who-signs question, the business review invitation. Routine renewal emails wait in Renewal Prep, where waiting, scheduled, sent and stopped are always in view. Nothing reaches a customer until someone approves it. When a routine step has earned your trust, you can let it run on its own, and take that back any time. Key accounts, and anything about price or contract wording, stay with a person.

6. It runs your playbook

Write your renewal steps once: check-ins, business reviews, the who-signs question, notice-window reminders for auto-renewing contracts, a thank-you after renewal. For each step choose off, remind me, draft for me, or send once trusted. Companies set the defaults; teams can work their own way within them.

7. Nuva answers questions

Ask Nuva, the built-in assistant, how an account is doing and it answers with the facts: value, renewal date, risk, owner and next step, then the risk factors and recent activity behind them. It can draft a check-in, which waits for approval like everything else.

8. Managers see the whole book

The executive summary shows total value, revenue at risk, renewal rate and accounts that have gone quiet. This quarter's renewals are split by risk; an urgency chart places each at-risk account by time left; urgent actions show where to step in. The gaps view lists what is missing across every renewal, sorted by money at stake, so coaching goes where it matters.

Frequently asked questions

Does Renewal360 replace our CRM?

No. It reads your CRM and acts on it. The CRM stays your system of record.

Will it email our customers without asking?

No. Every draft waits for approval unless you choose to let a specific routine step run on its own. You can pause all outgoing email at any time.

Is our data used to train AI models?

No. We do not use customer data to train or fine-tune our own AI models. Details are in the privacy policy.
