To run day-to-day customer success in Renewal360: CSMs start each morning in the Action Center and clear replies, decisions and tasks; they approve routine drafts in Renewal Prep; they open at-risk accounts to read the reasons and act on the gaps; and managers use the executive summary and gaps view each week to decide where to step in. The playbook runs in the background for every account.

This guide turns the product into a working rhythm for a CS team. It assumes your renewals, inboxes and other tools are already connected; if not, start with the setup guide.

What Renewal360 replaces

  • The renewal spreadsheet and its weekly update.
  • Calendar reminders for renewal dates and notice windows.
  • Writing check-ins, reminders and who-signs emails from scratch.
  • The Friday report on revenue at risk.

Your CRM stays the system of record; Renewal360 reads it and acts on it.

Every morning: the Action Center (CSMs)

  1. Customers waiting. Reply to customers who wrote to you first. Each row shows days left until their renewal.
  2. Decide. Clear the one-click choices.
  3. Do. Work through calls and tasks, starting with the accounts closest to renewal and most at risk.

Items close themselves when the work is done, for example when a customer has been answered.

Every day: approve drafts in Renewal Prep

Routine renewal emails the app has drafted wait here: waiting, scheduled, sent and stopped are always visible, and stopped emails say why. Edit, approve or reject. Nothing goes out until it is approved, unless you have let that specific step run on its own.

When an account is flagged: open the account view

  • Read where the deal stands and why the account was flagged.
  • Read the risk analysis: the reasons in plain English and how each factor scores.
  • Check the readiness questions: champion, contacts, signer, value, pricing, process, business review.
  • Use the timeline to see every email, meeting and risk update in one place.
  • Ask Nuva for a quick summary or a draft check-in.

Every week: the manager review

  • Executive summary: total value, revenue at risk, renewal rate, accounts that went quiet.
  • This quarter by risk: how much is safe and how much needs work.
  • Urgent actions: where a manager should step in first.
  • Gaps across the book: what is missing across every renewal, sorted by money at stake. Pick the top gaps to coach on.

Every month: tune the playbook

  • Look at which drafts were approved unchanged. Consider letting those steps run on their own.
  • Look at which steps get skipped or edited heavily, and adjust them.
  • Check that auto-renewing contracts have their notice-window steps.
  • Let teams adjust their own steps within the company's limits.

Frequently asked questions

How much time does Renewal360 take each day?

Most of the daily work is reviewing the Action Center and approving drafts. The aim is to spend less time on renewal admin and more on customer conversations.

Can managers set rules for their own teams?

Yes. The company sets the playbook and limits; teams can adjust their steps within those limits.

What do CSMs see compared with managers?

CSMs see their own accounts and work. Managers see their teams' accounts and the book-level views.
