The five customer success metrics most worth tracking for renewals are gross revenue retention (GRR), net revenue retention (NRR), time to first value, health score accuracy and revenue per CSM. One popular metric to stop using as a churn predictor on its own: raw NPS.

Most CS dashboards track too much. These five tell you whether customers stay, whether they grow, how fast they get value, whether your risk signals work, and whether your team can cover its book. Each section gives the formula and what to do with it.

Metric 1: Gross revenue retention (GRR)

What it is: the share of recurring revenue you keep from existing customers over a period, counting churn and downgrades but ignoring expansion.

Formula: (starting recurring revenue minus churned revenue minus downgrades) divided by starting recurring revenue. It can never exceed 100%.

Why it matters: GRR shows how leaky your base is. A healthy NRR can hide a poor GRR if a few customers expand while many leave. Watch both.

Metric 2: Net revenue retention (NRR)

What it is: the same calculation including expansion: upsells, cross-sells and price increases from existing customers.

Formula: (starting recurring revenue plus expansion minus churn minus downgrades) divided by starting recurring revenue.

Why it matters: above 100% means your existing customers grow faster than they leave. It is the clearest single sign that customers get increasing value from your product.

Metric 3: Time to first value

What it is: how long a new customer takes to reach their first meaningful result with your product. You define the milestone, for example first report shared or first workflow live.

Why it matters: customers who reach value slowly are much harder to renew. A first year that starts badly rarely ends with an easy renewal. Track it by segment and fix the slowest onboarding paths first.

Metric 4: Health score accuracy

What it is: how well your health or risk score predicted what actually happened. Of the accounts flagged at risk, how many churned or downgraded? Of the accounts that churned, how many were flagged in time?

Why it matters: a score nobody checks drifts into decoration. Review it every quarter against real outcomes and adjust the signals. A score that comes with written reasons is far easier to check than a bare number.

Metric 5: Revenue per CSM

What it is: the recurring revenue each CSM is responsible for, alongside the number of accounts and renewals they handle each quarter.

Why it matters: it shows whether your team can actually cover its book. If revenue per CSM rises while GRR falls, the team is stretched. Reducing admin work, such as tracking and writing routine emails, is usually cheaper than hiring.

The one to ignore: raw NPS as a churn predictor

NPS is useful for understanding sentiment, especially the comments. As a churn predictor on its own it is weak: the people who answer surveys are often not the people who decide renewals, response rates vary, and promoters still churn when budgets are cut or a champion leaves. Use NPS as one input next to engagement, usage, support and the renewal readiness questions, not as the answer.

What good looks like when all five work together

  • GRR and NRR reviewed monthly, by segment, with the reasons behind each loss written down.
  • Time to first value tracked for every new customer, with slow starts flagged early.
  • The risk score checked against outcomes every quarter.
  • Coverage per CSM visible, so workload problems show before renewals slip.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between GRR and NRR?

GRR counts only losses, churn and downgrades, so it cannot exceed 100%. NRR also counts expansion from existing customers, so it can go above 100%.

Which customer success metric matters most?

For renewals, GRR, because it shows how much of your base you keep. Pair it with NRR to see whether customers are growing.

Is NPS a good predictor of churn?

Not on its own. It reflects the people who answer surveys, who are often not the decision-makers. Combine it with engagement, usage and support signals.

How can we track these metrics without a data team?

GRR and NRR come from billing or CRM data. Risk, coverage and renewal status are easier with a renewal tool that reads your CRM, inbox and support desk, such as Renewal360's manager view.
